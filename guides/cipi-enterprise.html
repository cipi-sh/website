Cipi in the enterprise: where it fits, what it gives, what to add

Large organisations run more Laravel than they think: back-offices, portals, internal tools, client projects. Cipi gives them a standard, auditable way to host it on infrastructure they choose, without a vendor in the path.

By Andrea PollastriUpdated November 24, 20267 min read

Share

Enterprises usually meet Laravel through the side door: a Filament back-office here, a customer portal there, an agency delivering a project that now has to be hosted somewhere approved. Each lands on whatever the team found first. Cipi offers a standard way to host all of them, on infrastructure the organisation chooses, with the controls an auditor asks about built in.

Why enterprises look at self-hosting again#

DriverWhat it means for hosting
Data sovereigntyPrivacy law, sector rules (finance, health, public sector) and customer contracts increasingly specify where data lives and who can reach it. A server you choose, in a region you choose, with no third-party control plane, is the simplest answer
Regulation of ICT riskFrameworks such as NIS2 and, for financial entities, DORA ask organisations to manage risks from their ICT suppliers. Every SaaS in the deploy path is one more supplier to assess
Cost controlPer-seat and usage-based tools grow with headcount and traffic. A fixed VPS price and a free tool don't (the numbers)
Vendor riskPrice changes, licence changes, acquisitions and outages at a vendor become your problem. An open source tool with no service behind it removes that dependency (licences)
Shadow ITTeams that can't get hosting quickly will find it themselves. A standard, approved path is the cure

Where Cipi fits#

Use caseHow Cipi is used
Internal tools and back-officesMany Laravel/Filament apps on a few servers, each isolated, behind SSO or Cloudflare Access
Customer portals and APIsProduction on dedicated servers, staging on shared ones, deploys from CI through scoped tokens
Agency and supplier projectsThe supplier pushes to Git; the organisation owns the server, the data and the deploy history
Regulated dataServers in the required country or provider; backups encrypted on the server before they leave, to a bucket you choose
Business units with their own budgetsOne server (or more) per unit, all visible in one panel with roles
Legacy PHP next to LaravelWordPress, Symfony and other PHP as isolated custom apps, patched like the rest

The controls auditors ask about#

What Cipi does out of the box, grouped the way security questionnaires and ISO 27001 / SOC 2 audits group it:

AreaWhat Cipi provides
Identity and accessKey-only SSH for the admin user, root login off, alerts on logins and sudo. Panel with SSO over OpenID Connect (Entra ID, Okta, Google Workspace, Keycloak…), two-factor, roles per server (View, Manage) and per app (View, Deploy, Edit, Environment, Logs, Commands, Delete), single-use invite links
Least privilege for automationAPI tokens with listed abilities (deploy-manage alone deploys) and expiry dates, behind an IP allowlist. The API calls an allowlist of cipi commands through sudo; destructive host operations stay on the CLI
SegregationEach app is its own Linux user (home 750), PHP-FPM pool with open_basedir, database user and Valkey instance. A compromised app can't read the others
Change managementServer settings in cipi.yml, reviewed in pull requests, planned and applied all or nothing. A repository can't raise its own disk limit or touch another app's backups
Audit trailA hash-chained deploy ledger: every deploy, rollback and failure, with origin (CLI, panel, webhook, CI, AI agent, SSH), operator, IP and commit, verified by root and copied to syslog
Vulnerability managementUbuntu security updates daily; PHP, Nginx, databases and Valkey patched nightly, one service at a time; optional ClamAV scans and CrowdSec; Hackly and Checkpoint for the code
NetworkUFW allowing 22, 80 and 443 only, Fail2ban, optional Cloudflare Zero Trust that closes the origin's ports entirely
SecretsConfiguration, tokens and passwords encrypted at rest (AES-256), readable by root only; backups and sync archives encrypted before they leave
Backup and recoveryBackup profiles to any S3-compatible storage, encrypted, verified, with staleness alerts; warm standby servers with cipi sync
MonitoringServer monitor and health checks with alerts to e-mail, Slack, Discord, Google Chat, Telegram, signed webhooks and more; metrics kept by the minute; OpenMetrics for your Prometheus
PrivacyFixed log retention: logs with IP addresses 90 days, application and security logs 12 months; anonymized database dumps for developers
Evidencecipi compliance report: 17 controls checked read-only and mapped to ISO 27001:2022 Annex A and SOC 2, with raw evidence and checksums
bash
sudo cipi compliance                    # pass / warn / fail per control
sudo cipi compliance report --days=365  # bundle for the auditor
sudo cipi compliance controls           # mapping to ISO 27001 and SOC 2
sudo cipi deploy shop --audit --days=365 --json

Record the checksum of each compliance bundle outside the server (a ticket, the GRC tool). It proves later that the bundle wasn't altered.

What to add around it#

Being honest about the gaps is what makes a rollout succeed:

GapHow enterprises fill it
No vendor support or SLAName an internal platform owner (two, for holidays) and keep a runbook; or contract a local partner who knows Linux and Laravel. The stack is standard, so any experienced Linux administrator can work on it
No autoscaling or clusteringSize servers for peak; split heavy apps onto their own servers; keep a warm standby with cipi sync; put critical data on a managed database if you need automatic failover
Logs stay on the serverForward syslog to the SIEM (Splunk, Elastic, Microsoft Sentinel) with your usual agent; the compliance report checks that forwarding is configured
OIDC, not SAMLMost identity providers offer OIDC next to SAML for the same users (Entra ID, Okta, Google Workspace)
Ubuntu LTS onlyTreat it as an appliance OS: Ubuntu 24.04 or 26.04 on full VMs, nothing else installed by hand
Public IPv4 neededRequired for certificates and webhooks; after setup, cipi zt lock closes 22, 80 and 443 to the internet and traffic arrives through a Cloudflare Tunnel
Certifications for the tool itselfYour controls are audited, not the tool. Use a certified infrastructure provider for the VMs and cipi compliance for the evidence on top

Answers for the vendor questionnaire#

Procurement and security teams ask the same questions of every supplier. For Cipi:

QuestionAnswer
Does the software send data to the vendor?No. No telemetry, no account, no control plane. Alerts and backups go only to destinations you configure
Where is data stored?On your servers, and in the backup buckets you choose. Backups are encrypted on the server before upload
Who can access the servers?Your accounts only: SSH keys you add, panel users you invite, tokens you create
Licence and cost?MIT for every component; no licence fee, no per-seat or per-server pricing
Is the source available?Yes, all of it, on GitHub. What runs as root is readable on the server
How are vulnerabilities reported and fixed?Private reports through GitHub's vulnerability reporting (security.txt); fixes ship in the latest release, installed with cipi self-update
How are changes documented?A detailed changelog per release, with the tests each release adds
What if the project is discontinued?Servers keep working without it; the code can be forked; the server is standard Ubuntu
Can we exit?Yes: apps are ordinary Laravel apps, data is in standard MariaDB, MySQL or PostgreSQL, files in standard directories

Roll it out in five steps#

Step 1 of 5Pilot

Pick an internal tool with real users but limited impact. Install Cipi on a fresh VM, deploy it, run it for a month. Measure deploy time, incidents and hours spent.

Step 2 of 5Baseline

Write down the standard: Ubuntu version, VM size, Cipi version, backup destination and profiles, notification channels, CrowdSec on or off, Zero Trust on or off. Script it: wget -O - https://cipi.sh/setup.sh | SSH_PUBKEY="…" bash, then the same cipi commands every time.

Step 3 of 5Guardrails

Panel with SSO and roles; API tokens per pipeline with only the abilities they need and an expiry date; branch protection on repositories with cipi.yml; cipi yml auto only where pushing to main is already controlled.

Step 4 of 5Evidence

Schedule cipi compliance --json (exit 1 on any failure) and a monthly cipi compliance report; forward syslog; keep the bundles' checksums in your GRC tool.

Step 5 of 5Scale

Add servers per business unit or per environment, all in one panel. Use cipi-cli profiles and the PHP SDK for automation, and cipi sync to rebalance or move providers.

Questions

Is there a commercial support contract?

Cipi is MIT-licensed open source without a paid support desk. Enterprises typically pair it with an internal platform owner or a local partner who knows Linux and Laravel. See what to add.

Does Cipi have a SOC 2 report or an ISO 27001 certificate?

Certifications apply to organisations and services, and Cipi is software you run. What it gives you is evidence for your controls: cipi compliance report maps checks to ISO 27001:2022 Annex A and SOC 2.

Can it run on-premises?

On full virtual machines or bare metal (KVM, VMware, Hyper-V, Xen HVM) with Ubuntu 24.04 or 26.04, a public IPv4 address and ports 22, 80 and 443 reachable. Cloudflare Zero Trust can later close those ports to the internet.

Does it support SAML?

The panel supports OpenID Connect (Microsoft Entra ID, Okta, Google Workspace, Keycloak, Authentik, Zitadel, Auth0). SAML isn't supported.

Can we restrict what developers and AI assistants can do?

Yes: per-server and per-app roles in the panel, API tokens limited to listed abilities with an expiry date, an IP allowlist for the API, and MCP tools that inherit the token's abilities.

Where do our logs go?

They stay on the server with fixed retention (90 days for logs with IP addresses, 12 months for application and security logs). Forward syslog to your SIEM; the deploy ledger is already written there.

What happens if the Cipi project stops?

Your servers keep running: nothing depends on a Cipi service. The code is MIT, so it can be forked, and the server is standard Ubuntu any administrator can take over.
